# TextPAIR alignment filtering

Scripts that filtered the raw output of TextPAIR sequence-alignment runs over French printed works, 1500–1800 (ModERN project, ERC grant 101043369), before the alignments were loaded into PostgreSQL. TextPAIR writes one JSON record per aligned passage pair (`alignments.jsonl.lz4`, with passages, positions, filenames, metadata and TextPAIR's own group id); each script here reads one such file and writes the next (`alignments_clean1.jsonl`, `…clean2.jsonl`, …) together with a reservoir sample of what it rejected.

The repository is a record of what was done, in order, and of what was later found to be wrong with it; it is published as method, not as a recommended recipe.

## The rules

| step | rule | threshold |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | phrase blocklist: reject if ANY entry occurs anywhere in either passage (Aho-Corasick, lower-cased) | `data/filter_new_phrases_cleaned.txt` |
| 1 | paratext position: both sides in the last 10% of their documents, or both in the first 10%, and both passages under 100 words | 90 / 10 |
| 1 | passage too short | under 5 words |
| 1 | OCR noise: share of non-space characters that are letters, on the worse side | under 0.75 |
| 2 | group banality: drop every alignment of a TextPAIR group with this many members or more | 20 (June), 30 (August) |
| 3 | theatre markers (`ACTE II`, `SCÈNE III`, `Fin du premier acte`) in either passage | — |
| 4 | excluded filenames: legal compilations and customary-law collections aligned more than this many times | 234 → `data/excluded_filenames.json` |
| 5–6 | title echo and citation clutter; content density (fragments, name lists, dates) | `run_filter_pipeline.py` only |

The June pass also discarded same-author alignments; the August pipeline does not, since authors legitimately reuse their own work.

## What was later found to be wrong

- **The blocklist acts on a single hit.** A list built from frequency cannot tell a chancery formula from a much-quoted line, since a famous quotation is frequent because it is quoted. Of 977 short entries read one by one, two were wrong: Acts 5:29 (*il vaut mieux obéir à Dieu qu'aux hommes*, which alone cost 448 alignments in one run, Bayle's *Commentaire philosophique* reaching Voltaire among them) and Virgil, *Aeneid* VI.454. Short entries carry the risk; a minimum entry length would have prevented both. The list is published as it was used.
- **The group cap removes the most-reused passages first.** Group size measures how often a passage was quoted, so step 2 dropped exactly the upper layer of reuse the project exists to study; in one probe, 304 of 694 Montaigne × Latin alignments that passed step 1 were removed by it. The losses were later re-read and recovered through the per-row gates in `recovery/filter_new_alignments.py` rather than by raising the cap.
- **The position rule assumes paratext at a document's edges.** A classical text is often the work and nothing else, so its false positives fall on ancient texts.

Recovery is a re-filter of the retained raw files, never a re-alignment: `restore_blocked_alignments.py` re-applies step 1 in full with named entries relaxed, and ingests what survives as new rows (dry run by default).

## 1. June 2026 — the first pass

- `june/filter_modern_gallica.py` — All-in-one filter for alignments_mod.jsonl.lz4 (modern × Gallica corpus).
- `june/filter_ocr.py` — Remove OCR-noise alignments from alignments_clean.jsonl. Criterion: min(alpha_ratio(source_passage), alpha_ratio(target_passage)) < 0.75 Alpha ratio = fraction of non-space characters that are letters [a-zA-ZÀ-ÿ].
- `june/filter_banality.py` — Remove high-frequency group alignments (boilerplate / formulaic text). Criterion: group_id appears >= 20 times in the clean corpus. Two-pass: count group sizes, then filter.
- `june/filter_banality_gallica.py` — Remove high-frequency group alignments (boilerplate / formulaic text). Criterion: group_id appears >= 20 times in the clean corpus.
- `june/filter_theatre.py` — Remove theatrical structural markers from aligned passages. Rejects any alignment where source OR target passage contains act/scene markers (e.g. "ACTE II", "SCÈNE III", "Fin du premier Acte").
- `june/filter_source_freq.py` — Step 1 — Gallica corpus:   Count source_filename frequencies; remove alignments where   source_filename appears > MAX_FREQ times.   Saves the excluded filename list for use in filter_target_freq.py.
- `june/filter_target_freq.py` — Step 2 — modern × Gallica corpus:   Apply the excluded filename list generated by filter_source_freq.py,   this time checking target_filename (Gallica texts appear as targets here).
- `june/sampler_modern_gallica.py` — Random sample of clean alignments — produces a self-contained HTML viewer. Run on server, scp the HTML back for inspection.

## 2. August 2026 — the unified pipeline

- `pipeline/run_filter_pipeline.py` — Unified 4-step filter pipeline for any TextPAIR alignment corpus.
- `pipeline/run_filters_cleaned.sh` — Full filter pipeline for the cleaned modern × Gallica corpus.
- `pipeline/filter_cleaned_step1.py` — Step 1 — cleaned modern × Gallica corpus (477,501 alignments). Filters: phrase blocklist, position paratext, passage too short, OCR noise. Same-author intentionally skipped (Voltaire et al. self-plagiarise legitimately).
- `pipeline/filter_cleaned_step2.py` — Step 2 — group-size banality filter. Removes alignments whose group_id appears >= 20 times (formulaic boilerplate).
- `pipeline/filter_cleaned_step3.py` — Step 3 — theatrical act/scene marker filter. Rejects any alignment where source OR target passage contains act/scene markers (e.g. "ACTE II", "SCÈNE III", "Fin du premier Acte").
- `pipeline/filter_cleaned_step4.py` — Step 4 — target filename frequency filter. Removes alignments whose target_filename is in the existing excluded list (legal compilations, customary law collections, etc. — 95 filenames, threshold 234).

## Shared modules (imported by the recovery tools)

- `lib/boilerplate_patterns.py` — Shared vocabulary for classifying an identical-passage cluster as administrative boilerplate. Imported by both merge_identical_clusters.py and apply_boilerplate_exclusions.py so the two cannot drift apart: every cluster the merge script skips is exactly one the deletion script considers, and vice versa.
- `lib/noise_features.py` — One place that knows how a row becomes a feature vector. Import it; do not re-derive it.
- `lib/latin_detect.py` — Scores a passage as Latin or French. Imported by find_orphan_latin_sources.py and cluster_orphan_latin.py.
- `lib/containment.py` — Shared module: trigram windowed containment, and the orthographic folding that early modern Latin needs before any of it means anything.
- `lib/corpus_source.py` — One place that knows what "the corpus" means. Import it; do not re-derive it.
- `lib/locus_test.py` — One place that knows what "these two documents share a locus" means.

## Data

- `data/filter_new_phrases_cleaned.txt` — 9,942 lines
- `data/filter_new_phrases.txt` — 8,986 lines
- `data/excluded_filenames.json` — 95 entries

The phrase lists hold period boilerplate (printers' privileges, approbations, formulae), not corpus text. `excluded_filenames.json` names the Gallica files excluded by step 4; their PhiloLogic paths are given as `/path/to/philologic/...`, so replace that prefix with your own before running step 4, which matches the full path.

## Running

Python 3 with `lz4` and `pyahocorasick` for the filters; the recovery tools also need `psycopg2`, `numpy` and a PostgreSQL database in the TextPAIR schema, with connection settings read from TextPAIR's own configuration file (`/etc/text-pair/global_settings.ini`), never from the code. Put `lib/` and `pipeline/` on `PYTHONPATH`. `filter_new_alignments.py` expects a trained noise model (`noise_model.pkl`), which is not included. Paths are given as `/path/to/alignements/...`; the June scripts hard-code theirs and need editing before use.

    python3 pipeline/run_filter_pipeline.py /path/to/alignements/<run>/output/results
